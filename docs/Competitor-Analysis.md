# Competitive Landscape

## Overview

The project materials include a qualitative read of the Tunisian competitive environment rather than a full benchmarking matrix (pricing, footprint, and market-share data for each named competitor were not part of the delivered scope). What follows is what the team actually documented.

## Competitive Structure

| Layer | Description | Strategic Implication |
|---|---|---|
| International fast-food chains | Limited presence in Tunisia | Opening for first-mover advantage in premium fast-casual |
| Local/non-traditional fast-food competitors | Named examples: **Baguette et Baguette**, **Hobo** | Used as the competitive benchmark for core menu pricing |
| Street food & informal vendors | Deep-rooted, low fixed-cost, high cultural trust | Sets the low end of the price expectation band; source of menu inspiration (fricassée, brik, mtabga) |
| Affordable cafés | Hearty meals at accessible prices | Competes on the "everyday meal" occasion |

The strategy deliberately positions McTounsi **slightly above street food, but below premium/global competitors**, while claiming a *high* local-authenticity score through menu localization — a positioning space the team identified as currently uncontested.

## Why This Matters for Pricing

The team explicitly used local competitors as a **pricing anchor** rather than McDonald's global price architecture:

> *"Set prices using a Competitive Benchmark. Price the core menu (e.g., Big Mac, McChicken) slightly below or at par with the most popular, high-quality, non-traditional Tunisian fast-food competitors."*

See full detail in [Marketing Mix — Price](Marketing-Mix.md#price).

## Data Availability Note

No market-share figures, competitor unit-economics, or a full competitor comparison table were included in the source materials — these are flagged here rather than invented.
